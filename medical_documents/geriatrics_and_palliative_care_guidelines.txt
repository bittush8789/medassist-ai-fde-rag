SECTION 1: BEERS CRITERIA FOR POTENTIALLY INAPPROPRIATE MEDICATIONS (PIMs)

1.1 High-Risk Medication Classes to Avoid in Older Adults (Age >= 65)
- First-Generation Antihistamines (Diphenhydramine, Hydroxyzine): Highly anticholinergic; cause urinary retention, confusion, constipation, and significant fall risk.
- Benzodiazepines & Non-Benzodiazepine Sedatives (Diazepam, Lorazepam, Zolpidem): Increased risk of delirium, motor vehicle crashes, and hip fractures.
- Long-Acting Sulfonylureas (Glyburide, Glimepiride): High risk of prolonged and fatal hypoglycemia due to delayed renal clearance. Preferred oral agent in older adults is Metformin or DPP-4 inhibitors (Linagliptin).
- Antipsychotics (Haloperidol, Olanzapine, Quetiapine): Avoid for behavioral symptoms of dementia due to increased mortality (Black Box Warning) and cerebrovascular adverse events. Use non-pharmacological behavioral management first.
- Systemic NSAIDs: High risk of gastrointestinal ulceration, acute kidney injury, fluid retention, and worsening hypertension.

SECTION 2: ACUTE DELIRIUM EVALUATION & NON-PHARMACOLOGICAL MANAGEMENT

2.1 Confusion Assessment Method (CAM) Diagnostic Criteria (Requires 1 and 2, plus 3 or 4):
1. Acute Onset and Fluctuating Course.
2. Inattention (difficulty focusing or sustaining attention).
3. Disorganized Thinking (rambling or incoherent speech).
4. Altered Level of Consciousness (hyperalert, lethargic, stupor).

2.2 The 'PINCHES ME' Precipitating Causes Workup:
- Pain, Infection (UTI, Pneumonia), Nutrition, Constipation, Hydration/Hypoxia, Electrolytes/Endocrine, Stroke/Seizure, Medications (Anticholinergics/Opioids), Environment (ICU/sensory deprivation).
- Multicomponent Non-Pharmacological Bundle: Frequent re-orientation, daylight exposure, promoting sleep hygiene (minimize overnight vitals and alarms), early mobilization, hearing aids/glasses in place, and adequate hydration.

SECTION 3: PALLIATIVE CANCER PAIN MANAGEMENT & OPIOID CONVERSION

3.1 WHO Analgesic Ladder & Opioid Titration
- Step 1 (Mild Pain 1–3): Acetaminophen 500–1000 mg PO Q6H (Max 3000 mg/day in elderly/liver disease) or topical Lidocaine.
- Step 2 (Moderate Pain 4–6): Low-dose weak opioids (Tramadol, Codeine) or low-dose Morphine.
- Step 3 (Severe Pain 7–10): Strong opioids (Morphine, Oxycodone, Hydromorphone, Fentanyl).

3.2 Equianalgesic Opioid Conversion & Bowel Prophylaxis
- Morphine Equivalence: Oral Morphine 30 mg = Parenteral (IV/SC) Morphine 10 mg (Ratio 3:1) = Oral Oxycodone 20 mg = Oral Hydromorphone 7.5 mg = IV Hydromorphone 1.5 mg.
- Prophylactic Bowel Regimen (Mandatory for ALL patients on chronic opioids):
  * Stimulant Laxative (Senna 1–2 tablets PO daily to BID) PLUS Osmotic agent (Polyethylene Glycol 17 g daily). Bulk-forming fiber laxatives (Psyllium) are contraindicated due to risk of impaction.
