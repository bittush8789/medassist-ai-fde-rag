SECTION 1: DIABETIC KETOACIDOSIS (DKA) & HYPEROSMOLAR HYPERGLYCEMIC STATE (HHS)

1.1 Diagnostic Criteria for DKA
- Blood glucose > 250 mg/dL (13.9 mmol/L).
- Arterial or venous pH < 7.30.
- Serum bicarbonate < 18 mEq/L.
- Elevated serum or urine ketones (beta-hydroxybutyrate > 3.0 mmol/L).
- Anion gap > 12 mEq/L [Calculated as: Na - (Cl + HCO3)].

1.2 Stepwise DKA Resuscitation Protocol
1. Fluid Therapy:
   * 0.9% Normal Saline (0.9% NaCl) at 1000–1500 mL in the first hour.
   * Switch to 0.45% NaCl if corrected serum sodium is normal or high.
   * Add 5% Dextrose (D5W or D5 1/2NS) when blood glucose drops below 200 mg/dL to maintain glucose 150–200 mg/dL while continuing insulin until anion gap closes.
2. Insulin Infusion:
   * IV Regular Insulin continuous infusion at 0.1 units/kg/hour (or 0.14 units/kg/hour without bolus).
   * CRITICAL POTASSIUM RULE: If serum potassium < 3.3 mEq/L, HOLD insulin infusion until potassium is repleted to >= 3.3 mEq/L to prevent fatal cardiac arrhythmias.
3. Potassium Management:
   * If K+ is 3.3–5.2 mEq/L, add 20–30 mEq KCl per liter of IV fluid to maintain serum K+ between 4.0 and 5.0 mEq/L.
4. Resolution Criteria: Blood glucose < 200 mg/dL PLUS 2 of the following: serum bicarbonate >= 15 mEq/L, venous pH > 7.30, and anion gap <= 12 mEq/L. Administer basal SQ insulin 2 hours prior to stopping IV insulin.

SECTION 2: HYPOTHYROIDISM & MYXEDEMA COMA PROTOCOL

2.1 Outpatient Primary Hypothyroidism Replacement
- Levothyroxine (T4) Monotherapy:
  * Full replacement dose: 1.6 mcg/kg/day based on ideal body weight (e.g., 100–125 mcg daily).
  * Elderly patients or patients with known coronary artery disease: Start low at 25 to 50 mcg PO daily and titrate every 6 to 8 weeks based on serum TSH (Target TSH 0.5–2.5 mIU/L).
  * Administration instructions: Take in the morning on an empty stomach with a full glass of water, at least 30–60 minutes before breakfast (separate from calcium and iron supplements by >= 4 hours).

2.2 Myxedema Coma Emergency Protocol
- Presentation: Hypothermia, severe lethargy/coma, bradycardia, hypoventilation, hyponatremia.
- Immediate Emergency Pharmacotherapy:
  * Intravenous Stress-Dose Hydrocortisone: 100 mg IV bolus every 8 hours (Must be given BEFORE or simultaneously with thyroid hormone to avoid precipitating acute adrenal crisis).
  * Intravenous Levothyroxine: Loading dose 200–400 mcg IV bolus, followed by 50–100 mcg IV daily.
  * Consider IV Liothyronine (T3): 5–20 mcg IV loading dose for severe refractory cases.

SECTION 3: ACUTE ADRENAL CRISIS MANAGEMENT

3.1 Clinical Recognition
- Severe hypotension/refractory shock unresponsive to fluid boluses and vasopressors, fever, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, marked hyponatremia, hyperkalemia, and hypoglycemia.

3.2 Emergency Pharmacotherapy
1. Immediate Intravenous Hydrocortisone: 100 mg IV stat bolus, followed by 50 mg IV every 6 hours (or 200 mg/24 hours continuous infusion) for 24 to 48 hours until stabilized.
2. Rapid Fluid Resuscitation: 0.9% Normal Saline with 5% Dextrose (D5NS) at 1 L/hour in the first 1 to 2 hours to correct hypovolemia, hyponatremia, and hypoglycemia.
3. Mineralocorticoid coverage: High-dose Hydrocortisone (>= 100 mg/day) provides sufficient mineralocorticoid activity. Fludrocortisone (0.1 mg PO daily) is only added once Hydrocortisone dose is tapered below 50 mg/day.
