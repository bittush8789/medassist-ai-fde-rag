SECTION 1: VENOUS THROMBOEMBOLISM (DVT & PE) PHARMACOTHERAPY

1.1 Direct Oral Anticoagulants (DOACs) as First-Line Agents
DOACs are preferred over Vitamin K Antagonists (Warfarin) for non-valvular VTE:
- Apixaban (Eliquis):
  * Acute VTE Treatment: 10 mg PO twice daily (BID) for the first 7 days, followed by 5 mg PO twice daily for >= 3 to 6 months.
- Rivaroxaban (Xarelto):
  * Acute VTE Treatment: 15 mg PO twice daily with food for the first 21 days, followed by 20 mg PO once daily with the evening meal.
- Low-Molecular-Weight Heparin (LMWH, Enoxaparin):
  * Dose: 1 mg/kg subcutaneous every 12 hours (or 1.5 mg/kg SC once daily). Preferred in pregnancy, cancer-associated thrombosis (CAT), and severe hepatic impairment. Reduce dose to 1 mg/kg once daily if eGFR < 30 mL/min.

1.2 Duration of Anticoagulation
- Provoked VTE (Major transient risk factor such as surgery or trauma): 3 months.
- Unprovoked VTE or Recurrent VTE: Indefinite / extended anticoagulation if bleeding risk is acceptable.

SECTION 2: URGENT ANTICOAGULATION REVERSAL PROTOCOLS

2.1 Life-Threatening Bleeding Reversal Strategies
- Warfarin (Coumadin) Reversal:
  * 4-Factor Prothrombin Complex Concentrate (4F-PCC, Kcentra): Dosed by baseline INR (25–50 units/kg IV) PLUS Intravenous Vitamin K (Phytonadione 10 mg slow IV infusion over 30 min).
- Direct Thrombin Inhibitor (Dabigatran / Pradaxa) Reversal:
  * Idarucizumab (Praxbind): 5 g IV administered as two consecutive 2.5 g IV infusions.
- Factor Xa Inhibitors (Apixaban & Rivaroxaban) Reversal:
  * Andexanet alfa (Andexxa) IV infusion OR 4F-PCC (50 units/kg IV stat).

SECTION 3: IMMUNE THROMBOCYTOPENIA (ITP) & HEPARIN-INDUCED THROMBOCYTOPENIA (HIT)

3.1 Primary Immune Thrombocytopenia (ITP) Initial Therapy
- Treatment Threshold: Platelet count < 30,000/mcL (< 30 x 10^9/L) or any platelet count with active mucosal bleeding.
- First-Line Corticosteroid Regimens:
  * High-Dose Dexamethasone: 40 mg PO daily for 4 consecutive days (Preferred for rapid platelet response).
  * Oral Prednisone: 1.0 mg/kg/day for 14 to 21 days, then tapered.
- Intravenous Immunoglobulin (IVIG): 1.0 g/kg IV as a single dose (or 0.4 g/kg/day for 5 days) when rapid platelet elevation is required prior to emergency surgery or for life-threatening bleeding.

3.2 Heparin-Induced Thrombocytopenia (HIT - The 4T Score)
- Hallmarks: >= 50% drop in platelet count beginning 5 to 10 days after heparin exposure, or new thrombosis.
- Action: STOP all unfractionated and low-molecular-weight heparin immediately (including flushes). Switch to a non-heparin direct thrombin inhibitor: Argatroban continuous IV infusion (titrated to aPTT 1.5–3.0 times baseline) or Bivalirudin.
