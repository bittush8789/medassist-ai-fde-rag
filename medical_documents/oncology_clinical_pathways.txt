Section 1: Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor (ICI) Toxicities and Management
Document: oncology_clinical_pathways.txt | Clinical Oncology Protocols

Immune-Mediated Adverse Reactions (irAEs):
Checkpoint inhibitors (anti-PD-1, anti-PD-L1, anti-CTLA-4) can cause autoimmune toxicities affecting multiple organ systems.

Grading and Therapeutic Algorithms:
1. Grade 1 (Mild symptoms, asymptomatic lab abnormalities): Continue ICI therapy with close clinical monitoring.
2. Grade 2 (Moderate symptoms, limiting age-appropriate instrumental ADLs):
   - Withhold ICI temporarily.
   - Initiate oral corticosteroids: Prednisone 0.5 to 1 mg/kg/day (or equivalent).
   - Taper steroids slowly over at least 4 to 6 weeks once symptoms improve to Grade <= 1.
3. Grade 3-4 (Severe or life-threatening symptoms):
   - Permanently discontinue ICI (or withhold select Grade 3 until multidisciplinary review).
   - Hospitalize patient immediately.
   - High-dose systemic corticosteroids: Intravenous Methylprednisolone 1 to 2 mg/kg/day.
   - Steroid-Refractory Cases (no improvement after 48-72 hours): Add targeted immunosuppression: Infliximab (5 mg/kg) for immune-mediated colitis; Mycophenolate Mofetil (500-1000 mg twice daily) for immune-mediated hepatitis. Infliximab is contraindicated in immune hepatitis due to hepatotoxicity risk.

Section 2: Febrile Neutropenia Assessment and Empiric Antimicrobial Protocols
Document: oncology_clinical_pathways.txt | Clinical Oncology Protocols

Clinical Definition of Febrile Neutropenia:
- Fever: A single oral temperature measurement of >= 38.3°C (101°F) or a sustained temperature >= 38.0°C (100.4°F) over a 1-hour period.
- Neutropenia: Absolute Neutrophil Count (ANC) < 500 cells/mcL, or an ANC expected to decrease to < 500 cells/mcL within 48 hours.

Immediate Empiric Therapy (Door-to-Antibiotic time < 60 minutes):
1. Monotherapy Antipseudomonal Beta-Lactams:
   - Cefepime 2 grams IV every 8 hours, OR
   - Piperacillin-Tazobactam (Zosyn) 4.5 grams IV every 6 hours (or 3.375g over 4h extended infusion), OR
   - Meropenem 1 gram IV every 8 hours.
2. Indications for Adding Vancomycin:
   - Hemodynamic instability / septic shock.
   - Suspected catheter-related blood stream infection.
   - Mucositis Grade >= 3.
   - Known MRSA colonization.
   - Apparent skin or soft tissue infection.
