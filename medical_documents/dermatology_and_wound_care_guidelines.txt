SECTION 1: SEVERE CUTANEOUS ADVERSE REACTIONS (SJS & TEN)

1.1 Clinical Differentiation & Culprit Drugs
- Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS): Epidermal detachment < 10% total Body Surface Area (BSA) with mucosal membrane involvement in >= 2 sites (oral, ocular, urogenital).
- SJS/TEN Overlap: 10% to 30% BSA detachment.
- Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN): > 30% BSA epidermal sloughing with positive Nikolsky sign.
- High-Risk Culprit Drugs: Allopurinol, Aromatic Anticonvulsants (Carbamazepine, Phenytoin, Lamotrigine), Sulfonamides (TMP-SMX), Nevirapine, and Oxicam NSAIDs (Meloxicam, Piroxicam).

1.2 Emergency Management & SCORTEN Prognostic Score
- Immediate Actions:
  * Immediately withdraw ALL suspected and newly initiated medications.
  * Transfer urgently to an Intensive Care Unit (ICU) or specialized Burn Center.
  * Avoid aggressive debridement; provide sterile non-adherent silicone dressings, aggressive fluid replacement, and urgent ophthalmology consultation for amniotic membrane grafting.
- SCORTEN Predictors of Mortality (1 point each): Age >= 40, Heart rate >= 120 bpm, Malignancy, BSA detached > 10%, Serum Urea > 28 mg/dL, Serum Glucose > 252 mg/dL, Serum Bicarbonate < 20 mEq/L.

SECTION 2: CELLULITIS & ERYSIPELAS ANTIMICROBIAL REGIMENS

2.1 Non-Purulent vs Purulent Cellulitis Stratification
- Non-Purulent Cellulitis (Streptococcus pyogenes predominance):
  * Mild / Outpatient: Cephalexin 500 mg PO four times daily (QID) for 5 to 7 days, OR Amoxicillin 500 mg PO TID. Penicillin allergy: Clindamycin 300–450 mg PO QID.
  * Moderate / Inpatient: Intravenous Cefazolin 1–2 g IV every 8 hours or Ceftriaxone 1–2 g IV daily.
- Purulent Cellulitis / Suspected CA-MRSA (Staphylococcus aureus predominance):
  * Outpatient: Trimethoprim-Sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX) 1–2 DS tablets PO BID for 5 to 10 days, OR Doxycycline 100 mg PO BID.
  * Inpatient / Severe: Intravenous Vancomycin (15–20 mg/kg IV every 8–12 hours, target trough 15–20 mcg/mL) or Daptomycin 6–8 mg/kg IV daily.

SECTION 3: PRESSURE INJURY STAGING & WOUND DRESSING SELECTION

3.1 NPUAP Pressure Injury Stages
- Stage 1: Non-blanchable erythema of intact skin over a bony prominence.
- Stage 2: Partial-thickness skin loss with exposed dermis (shallow open ulcer with red/pink wound bed or intact/ruptured serum-filled blister).
- Stage 3: Full-thickness skin loss; adipose tissue is visible, but bone, tendon, or muscle are not exposed.
- Stage 4: Full-thickness skin and tissue loss with directly exposed or palpable fascia, muscle, tendon, ligament, or bone.
- Unstageable: Full-thickness tissue loss in which base is completely obscured by slough (yellow/tan) or eschar (brown/black).

3.2 Evidence-Based Dressing Selection
- Exudative / Heavily Draining Wounds: Calcium Alginate dressings or Hydrofiber (absorbs up to 20 times its weight).
- Dry / Necrotic Wounds Requiring Autolytic Debridement: Amorphous Hydrogel dressings to donate moisture.
- Light-to-Moderate Drainage & Epithelialization: Hydrocolloid dressings or Foam dressings.
